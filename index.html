Lighthouse Spokane

A gathering point for curious minds working through the philosophy, spirituality, and practice of a genuinely uncertain moment — together.

Background

Lighthouse Spokane sits at the intersection of a few different threads, and it's worth naming each of them.

The Liminal Web is a loose, informal network of thinkers, practitioners, and communities exploring what comes after modernity and postmodernism — people asking how we make sense of a moment defined by rapid change, competing worldviews, and a search for meaning that doesn't fit neatly into old categories. It isn't a single philosophy or institution; it's more like a shared conversation happening across many disciplines — psychology, education, spirituality, systems thinking — among people trying to think clearly about disorientation without pretending it isn't there.

Unitarian Universalism (UU) is a faith tradition built on shared values rather than a fixed creed — covenantal community, the inherent worth of every person, and a free and responsible search for truth and meaning drawn from many sources: science, philosophy, world religions, direct experience. UU congregations often become homes for exactly this kind of open inquiry, which is part of why this project found a natural partner there.

The UUA's New Communities Partnership Program provides support for new, experimental forms of spiritual community — groups that don't necessarily look like a traditional congregation but are doing similar work: gathering people around shared inquiry, practice, and mutual support. Lighthouse Spokane exists because of a grant through this program via the Inland Northwest Unitarian Universalist Community. Learn more about the UUA New Communities Partnership Program →

What we're attempting: Liminal philosophy and contemplative spiritual practice can be dense — dense enough that they often stay confined to academic circles or established spiritual communities with their own barriers to entry. Lighthouse Spokane exists to take that material and make it approachable for curious people in Spokane who want to think seriously about the moment we're in, without needing a philosophy degree or a pre-existing spiritual practice to participate. The goal isn't to hand people conclusions — it's to build an actual community of practice: a group of people who keep showing up, keep thinking together, and slowly develop shared language and tools for navigating uncertain terrain.

In Partnership With

Lighthouse Spokane is co-presented by the Inland Northwest Unitarian Universalist Community and Clear Sky Center, a meditation and intentional-community center whose teachers help shape our contemplative practice sessions.

About

Format

Lighthouse Spokane meets roughly once a month, in person in Spokane with a remote option for those joining online. Each gathering blends a few elements: some kind of grounding or movement practice to start, a short piece of teaching or input — a video, a reading, a guest presentation — small-group conversation, and a closing reflection.

Focus

Sessions draw from metamodern philosophy, contemplative practice, and voices working at the edges of psychology, education, and spirituality — the kind of material found across the liminal web. Topics have ranged from how we relate to different kinds of knowing, to navigating rupture and repair in relationships, to sitting with grief about the state of the world without collapsing into despair or forcing false optimism.

Approach

Nothing here is delivered as a finished answer. Each gathering is treated as a live, collective inquiry — the group studying ideas together and then talking honestly about how those ideas actually land in daily life. The structure stays loose on purpose, shaped in real time by whoever shows up and what they bring.

Who It's For

Lighthouse Spokane is for people who sense something is shifting — in themselves, in their relationships, in the wider world — and want company while they think it through. Some participants come with a long meditation or philosophy background; many don't. Some are lifelong seekers; others are simply tired of holding everything alone and looking for people to think alongside.

What To Expect

You do not need to have read anything, practiced anything, or prepared in any way. Come as you are. Every session is designed to be a legitimate entry point on its own — you don't need to have attended the ones before it to follow along or contribute.

An Invitation

This group is still taking shape, and it's meant to be shaped by the people in the room, not handed down from the front. If you have a reading, a practice, a framework, or simply a question you'd like to bring to the group, we want to hear about it — co-creating the material together is the whole point.

Catherine Pawasarat was a student of metaphysics, Western spiritual traditions, and the ayahuasca sacraments in the 1990s. Living in Kyoto, Japan for 20 years, she worked as an advocacy photojournalist specializing in environmental and human rights, and edited sustainability-related publications for various United Nations bodies. She also studied traditional Japanese arts.

Since 1998 she has trained daily with Buddhist teacher Achariya Doug Duncan (Qapel) in an intensive spiritual apprenticeship that's rare in the modern West. Transmitted from the remarkable Namgyal Rinpoche, they are both lineage holders of these teachings.

With Qapel, she is co-founder and resident teacher at Clear Sky Retreat Center in the British Columbia Rockies. There Catherine Sensei has spearheaded an innovative and sustainability-oriented culture and organization, specializing in conscious community. Together Doug and Catherine also teach through a virtual vehicle, Planet Dharma. In 2018 they wrote the best-selling book Wasteland to Pureland.

Catherine Sensei is an expert in traditional Japanese culture, and the author of the first comprehensive English guidebook to Kyoto's 1150-year-old Gion Festival, a gigantic living collection of diverse spiritual rituals. She also teaches and advocates for the unique role and experiences of Women in Buddhism.

Karen McAllister's work brings together two worlds often kept apart — money and meaning. She supports people in exploring not just what they do with money, but how they relate to it emotionally, psychologically, and spiritually. Blending practical financial guidance with reflective inquiry, she helps uncover the patterns and beliefs that shape financial life and sense of purpose.

Karen is a founding member, resident, and co-executive director of Clear Sky Retreat Center in British Columbia, a living lab for collective wisdom. She brings over 35 years of meditation practice and more than 20 years of study with the center's founding teachers, Qapel and Catherine Pawasarat Sensei.

Duncan Cryle is a founding member, resident, and co-executive director of Clear Sky Retreat Center in British Columbia. He has 35+ years of meditation practice, 20 years of intensive study with Clear Sky's founding teachers Qapel and Catherine Pawasarat Sensei, and has co-developed an integral community as a "living lab" for collective wisdom.

In a parallel life, he pioneered workplace mindfulness programs at IBM and SS&C Technologies, and is the founder and consultant at Conscious Organizations, working to help heart-centred teams and organizations build trust and collaboration.
